CosmoFit

A Python library for cosmological parameter estimation and Bayesian inference. Since v2.0 it is built from components (theories that compute, likelihoods that compare, samplers that explore), assembled from a YAML input, run with the cosmofit command and written as getdist chains. A wide bench of dark-energy models (ΛCDM, wCDM, CPL, JBP, BA, PEDE, GEDE, sign-switching Λ, Chaplygin gas, interacting DE) and modified gravity (f(R), f(R,T), f(T), f(Q), DGP) fitted down to structure growth; 21 datasets including DESI DR2 and the CMB through CAMB; Metropolis–Hastings, emcee, dynesty, profile and Fisher samplers; and a desktop GUI.

  • Python
  • MCMC
  • Modified gravity
  • YAML · CLI
  • PyPI
  • MIT

CosmoRecon

Model-independent reconstruction of H(z), dL(z) and fσ8(z), and then the question the field mostly does not answer: how much of that result is the method rather than the measurement? A reconstruction is carried as a posterior over functions, as draws rather than a curve with error bars, so correlations survive every null test: Om, curvature, distance duality, litmus, growth–geometry. Significance is computed in the eigenbasis of the statistic's own covariance with the effective degrees of freedom measured rather than counted, and a method ensemble splits a published error bar into the part the data measured and the part the method chose.

  • Python
  • Gaussian processes
  • Null tests
  • Pre-alpha
  • MIT

Cosmos

An interactive desktop application that teaches cosmology from the size of the universe to the open problems at the frontier. 58 lessons across 8 levels, each readable two ways (the story in words, or every formula and derivation), and 29 simulators standing on real data: Pantheon+ supernovae, SPARC rotation curves, the WMAP sky, an SDSS slice. Quizzes with spaced repetition, flashcards, worked problem sets, a 206-term glossary, classroom mode, and an export of the whole course as a static website, all on a physics engine verified against astropy. That export is hosted here: the lessons, quizzes, glossary, formula sheet and problems read in the browser, with the simulators and progress tracking living in the desktop app.

wljs-gr-toolkit

Thirteen Wolfram Language notebooks for symbolic general relativity and modified gravity. A metric goes in and curvature tensors come out; an action goes in and field equations, Friedmann equations and E(z) come out, then distances, growth, Geff, the teleparallel families, a Hamiltonian mode count, what the quasi-static approximation costs, gravitational-wave propagation, the Solar System bound derived rather than quoted, and which of the three families a standard siren could actually catch. Every notebook ends in verification cells against known limits.

  • Wolfram Language
  • WLJS Notebook
  • f(R) · f(T) · f(Q)

string-th

A simulation toolkit for string theory. It solves the worldsheet equations numerically (bosons by leapfrog, fermions by Lax–Wendroff), counts the quantum states, and reproduces the classic results as computed output rather than quoted facts: the critical dimension, the Regge trajectory, T-duality, orbifold twisted sectors, all five superstring theories and the two heterotic lattices. It starts with the bosonic string and each layer is required to reproduce the one below it. Beyond the spectrum: the Hagedorn temperature, mirror symmetry as a polytope exchanged with its dual, Green–Schwarz anomaly cancellation picking out its 496, and D-branes from the BIon spike to the Myers effect. Anything checkable two ways is checked two ways: 1543 checks in the test suite.

  • Python

  • Superstrings
  • Heterotic
  • D-branes

qwen3.6-35b-a3b-rft

Rejection sampling fine-tuning of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B for math, physics, tool calling and agentic Python, on a Google Colab budget. The model solves problems with verifiable answers, graders keep the correct solutions, and the shortest correct one per problem becomes training data for a LoRA. Evaluated against the base model under identical conditions (GPQA Diamond physics from 55% to 63%, MATH-500 with a Python tool from 61% to 71%), then exported as a mixed Q4 GGUF that runs locally in LM Studio.
