I work on the background cosmology of gravity theories that are not general relativity — f(R), f(R,T), f(T), f(Q), Gauss–Bonnet — and on the machinery needed to take them seriously: symbolic field equations on one end, Bayesian model comparison against real data on the other. Alongside that: string theory as something to simulate rather than quote, quantum algorithms, and fine-tuned language models for physics.

01 Research interests

Cosmology & dark energy

Expansion histories E(z), distance measures, the BAO ruler, structure growth and fσ8. Which dark-energy parametrisations are actually distinguishable by the data, and which only look distinguishable because of the shape that was assumed — which is also the case for reconstructing those functions with no model at all, where the method itself becomes part of the error budget.

  • ΛCDM
  • CPL / JBP / BA
  • PEDE · GEDE
  • Reconstruction
  • H₀ tension

Modified gravity

From an action to observables, symbolically. Curvature-based f(R), f(R,T), the teleparallel f(T) and symmetric-teleparallel f(Q) families, Gauss–Bonnet terms — plus the constraint analysis that says how many degrees of freedom a theory really propagates.

  • Field equations
  • Friedmann equations
  • Geff & slip
  • Mode counting

String theory

The string treated as something to compute with rather than quote: worldsheet dynamics and the Virasoro constraints, the light-cone spectrum, the critical dimension falling out of the conformal anomaly, compactification and T-duality, orbifolds, D-branes, and the superstring and heterotic constructions built on top of all of it.

  • Worldsheet
  • Light-cone quantisation
  • T-duality
  • Orbifolds
  • Superstrings

Bayesian inference

MCMC parameter estimation, convergence diagnostics, and honest model comparison (AIC/BIC, evidence) — because a modified-gravity model with three extra parameters fitting slightly better is not, on its own, news.

  • MCMC
  • Model selection
  • SNe · BAO · CC

Quantum computing

Reproducing quantum algorithms from the literature and running them on simulators and real backends — currently the quantum permutation algorithm of Yalçınkaya & Gedik (PRA 96, 062339).

  • Qiskit
  • Algorithm replication

Machine learning for physics

Fine-tuning open-weight LLMs and vision-language models on physics and mathematical notation: physics question answering, and handwritten-formula → LaTeX transcription.

  • LoRA / Unsloth
  • VLM OCR
  • Llama · Qwen

Scientific software

Research code that other people can actually run: packaged, tested, documented, and verified against limits where the answer is already known. A method that cannot recover general relativity cannot be trusted with anything else.

  • PyPI
  • CI
  • Reproducibility

CosmoRecon

—

Model-independent reconstruction of H(z), dL(z) and fσ8(z) — and then the question the field mostly does not answer: how much of that result is the method rather than the measurement? A reconstruction is carried as a posterior over functions, as draws rather than a curve with error bars, so correlations survive every null test — Om, curvature, distance duality, litmus, growth–geometry. Significance is computed in the eigenbasis of the statistic's own covariance with the effective degrees of freedom measured rather than counted, and a method ensemble splits a published error bar into the part the data measured and the part the method chose.

  • Python
  • Gaussian processes
  • Null tests
  • Pre-alpha
  • MIT

Cosmos

—

An interactive desktop application that teaches cosmology from the size of the universe to the open problems at the frontier. 53 lessons across 8 levels, each readable two ways — the story in words, or every formula and derivation — and 24 simulators standing on real data: Pantheon+ supernovae, SPARC rotation curves, the WMAP sky, an SDSS slice. Quizzes with spaced repetition, worked problem sets, a 184-term glossary, classroom mode and PDF printing, on a physics engine verified against astropy.

wljs-gr-toolkit

—

Nine Wolfram Language notebooks for symbolic general relativity and modified gravity. A metric goes in and curvature tensors come out; an action goes in and field equations, Friedmann equations and E(z) come out — then distances, growth, Geff, the teleparallel families, and a Hamiltonian mode count. Every notebook ends in verification cells against known limits.

  • WLJS Notebook

string-th

—

A simulation toolkit for string theory. It solves the worldsheet equations numerically — bosons by leapfrog, fermions by Lax–Wendroff — counts the quantum states, and reproduces the classic results as computed output rather than quoted facts: the critical dimension, the Regge trajectory, T-duality, orbifold twisted sectors, all five superstring theories and the two heterotic lattices. It starts with the bosonic string and each layer is required to reproduce the one below it. Anything checkable two ways is checked two ways.

  • Python
  • Worldsheet
  • Superstrings
  • Heterotic
  • Animations
  • MIT
