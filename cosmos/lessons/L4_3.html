L4.3 Big Bang Nucleosynthesis

In the first twenty minutes the universe was a nuclear reactor. It produced the hydrogen and helium that still make up almost all ordinary matter.

A cosmic nuclear reactor

About one second after the Big Bang the universe was at K and contained photons, neutrinos, electrons, positrons and a small sprinkling of protons and neutrons. Within the next twenty minutes some of these protons and neutrons fused into light nuclei. This process is called Big Bang nucleosynthesis (BBN).

A prediction confirmed

BBN predicts the amounts of helium-4, deuterium, helium-3 and lithium-7 using only well-tested nuclear physics and one unknown number: the density of ordinary matter. The agreement between prediction and observation is a cornerstone of the Big Bang theory.

Step 2: the deuterium bottleneck

The first step towards heavier nuclei is deuterium (one proton plus one neutron), bound by only 2.2 MeV. At first, high-energy photons break deuterium apart as soon as it forms.

Why wait so long?

The typical photon energy drops below 2.2 MeV after less than a minute, but there are about photons per baryon. Even when the average photon is too weak, the rare energetic photons in the tail of the blackbody spectrum are numerous enough to destroy deuterium. Why no heavier elements?

There are no stable nuclei with 5 or 8 nucleons, which blocks the easy routes to heavier elements. Moreover, the universe keeps cooling and thinning, so after about 20 minutes the nuclei no longer have enough energy to fuse. Only tiny traces of lithium-7 were made. All carbon, oxygen, iron and the other heavy elements were forged much later in stars.

Deuterium: a baryon density meter

Leftover deuterium is very sensitive to the density of ordinary matter: the more baryons, the more efficiently deuterium is burned into helium, so more baryons means less deuterium.

The lithium problem

Observed lithium-7 in old stars is about three times lower than BBN predicts. It is not yet clear whether this points to stellar physics that destroys lithium, measurement issues, or new physics.

  • The deuterium bottleneck delayed nucleosynthesis until about 3 minutes.

1. Why were there fewer neutrons than protons in the early universe?

The neutron–proton mass difference of 1.29 MeV suppresses neutrons by the Boltzmann factor exp(−Δmc²/kT).

2. What is the deuterium bottleneck?

With over a billion photons per baryon, enough photons can break the weakly bound deuterium until about 3 minutes.

3. If n/p = 1/7 when nucleosynthesis starts, what is the helium-4 mass fraction?

Yp = 2(n/p)/(1 + n/p) = (2/7)/(8/7) = 0.25.

4. Why did Big Bang nucleosynthesis produce almost no elements heavier than lithium?

5. If the universe contained more baryons, the leftover amount of deuterium would be...

A higher baryon density burns deuterium into helium more efficiently, leaving less behind. This makes deuterium a baryon density meter.
