Why only two equations?

Einstein's equations are ten equations in general. Symmetry reduces them to two independent ones: the time–time part and the three identical space–space parts.

Quiz

1. Which ingredients are inserted into Einstein's equations to derive the Friedmann equations?

Symmetry reduces the ten field equations to two independent Friedmann equations.

2. What is the meaning of k in the relativistic Friedmann equation?

In the Newtonian derivation k looked like an energy; general relativity shows it is spatial curvature.

3. In the acceleration equation, which combination acts as the source of gravity?

ä/a = −(4πG/3)(ρ + 3p/c²) + Λc²/3: pressure gravitates, and negative pressure can make gravity repulsive.

4. Which equation follows from conservation of energy and momentum, ∇_μ T^μν = 0?

It is the first law of thermodynamics for an expanding volume and gives ρ ∝ a^(−3(1+w)).

5. Why can a cosmological constant accelerate the expansion, which Newtonian gravity cannot explain?

In general relativity pressure contributes to gravity; strongly negative pressure produces repulsion.
