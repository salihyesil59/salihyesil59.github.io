L3.1 Radiation, Matter, Dark Energy and the Equation of State

Each ingredient of the universe dilutes in its own way as space expands. This decides which one dominates at each epoch.

Describing a cosmic fluid

On large scales every component of the universe behaves like a smooth fluid with an energy density and a pressure . Their ratio is the equation of state parameter

Three values are fundamental:

Component Pressure Examples
Matter ("dust") negligible atoms, dark matter, galaxies
Radiation positive photons, neutrinos in the early universe
Cosmological constant negative vacuum energy, dark energy

Quiz

Choose an answer to see whether it is right, and why. Score 70% or more and you know this lesson.

1. What is the equation of state parameter w for radiation?

Radiation has p = ρc²/3, so w = 1/3.

2. Why does radiation density fall as a⁻⁴ instead of a⁻³?

Dilution gives a⁻³, and redshift reduces each photon's energy by another factor of a.

3. How does the density of a cosmological constant change as the universe expands?

With w = −1, ρ ∝ a^(−3(1+w)) = a⁰: the density is constant.

4. In a flat universe dominated by matter, the scale factor grows as...

a ∝ t^(2/(3(1+w))) with w = 0 gives a ∝ t^(2/3).

5. What was the correct order of the dominant components in cosmic history?

Radiation (a⁻⁴) dominated first, then matter (a⁻³), and finally the constant dark energy density.
