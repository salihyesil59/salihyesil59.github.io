L0.2 Distance Units: AU, Light-Year, Parsec, Megaparsec

The units astronomers use for distances in the Solar System, the Galaxy and the universe, and how to convert between them.

Why new units?

Kilometres are fine on Earth, but the distance to the nearest star is about 40 000 000 000 000 km. Numbers like this are hard to read and easy to get wrong. Astronomers therefore use larger units, each suited to a particular scale.

A light-year is not a time

Despite the word "year", a light-year measures distance. Saying "the star is 4 light-years away" means light needs 4 years to reach us from it.

The parsec (pc)

Professional astronomers mostly use the parsec. It comes from the way the nearest star distances are measured: parallax.

As the Earth orbits the Sun, a nearby star seems to shift slightly against very distant background stars, just as your thumb seems to jump when you look at it with one eye and then the other. Half of that yearly shift is the parallax angle .

A star has a distance of 1 parsec if its parallax angle is exactly one arcsecond (1/3600 of a degree). Because the angle is tiny, the distance is simply inversely proportional to it:

Worked example

The nearest star to the Sun, Proxima Centauri, has a parallax of 0.768 arcseconds. Its distance is pc, or about light-years.

Kiloparsecs, megaparsecs and gigaparsecs

For larger distances we add the usual metric prefixes:

Unit Value Typical use
1 kpc (kiloparsec) 1 000 pc ≈ 3 260 ly Sizes of galaxies; the Sun is 8 kpc from the Galactic centre
1 Mpc (megaparsec) 1 000 000 pc ≈ 3.26 million ly Distances between galaxies; Andromeda is 0.78 Mpc away
1 Gpc (gigaparsec) 1 000 Mpc ≈ 3.26 billion ly Cosmological distances

The megaparsec is the unit of cosmology. You will meet it constantly, for example in the Hubble constant, measured in kilometres per second per megaparsec.

Remember this

  • AU ≈ 150 million km: distances inside the Solar System.
  • Light-year ≈ 9.46 trillion km: the distance light travels in a year.
  • Parsec ≈ 3.26 ly: defined through a parallax of one arcsecond.
  • Megaparsec ≈ 3.26 million ly: the everyday unit of cosmology.

1. What does a light-year measure?

A light-year is the distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m.

2. A star has a parallax angle of 0.1 arcseconds. How far away is it?

d [pc] = 1 / p [arcsec] = 1 / 0.1 = 10 parsecs.

3. Approximately how many light-years are there in one parsec?

1 pc ≈ 3.26 light-years. The number 206 265 is the number of AU in a parsec.

4. Which unit is most convenient for distances between galaxies?

Galaxies are typically millions of parsecs apart, so the megaparsec (Mpc) is the standard unit of cosmology.

5. If a star's parallax is smaller, the star is...

Distance is inversely proportional to parallax: a smaller apparent shift means a larger distance.
